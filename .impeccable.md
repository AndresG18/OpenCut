## Design Context

### Users

OpenCut serves creators who need to turn several imperfect recordings into a polished social video. They review repeated takes, keep only fluent sections, and compare alternate openings, middles, and closings before choosing a final cut.

### Brand Personality

Calm, capable, and direct. The editor should feel fast and professional without becoming dense or intimidating. The consequence of every action must be clear, especially the difference between reviewing media and changing a timeline.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Preserve the rewrite's compact, neutral, desktop-editor visual language and its existing theme tokens.
- Favor clear hierarchy, restrained contrast, and small purposeful controls over decorative effects.
- Make frequent review actions obvious; a creator should understand how to inspect a candidate before committing it.
- Use motion only for feedback or state change.
- Avoid ornamental gradients, excessive containers, and novelty interactions that slow editing.

### Design Principles

1. Review before committing: previewing a candidate must never add or alter timeline content.
2. Keep versions safe: alternate edits should be isolated so creators can compare them without overwriting an approved cut.
3. Make consequences legible: distinguish Preview from Add to timeline with explicit labels and state.
4. Optimize for repetition: reviewing many takes should be quick and low-friction.
5. Match the editor: new controls reuse existing components, spacing, and theme behavior.
